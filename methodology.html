What this is

A searchable full-text database of New York City municipal labor agreements: every agreement on the Office of Labor Relations (OLR) Recent Agreements page, plus 20 documents published elsewhere by OLR and the City University of New York (CUNY). Some agreements in force are not published anywhere; they are listed under known missing documents.

Why it exists

The city publishes its collective bargaining agreements as scattered PDFs. Many are scans of paper documents — the text isn't selectable, you can't even use Ctrl-F inside them. Comparing how, say, the discipline process works for sanitation workers versus teachers requires opening dozens of PDFs side by side. This site puts every clause from every contract in one searchable, taggable, citable place.

Sources

  • OLR Recent Agreements — the city's list of agreements from the current bargaining round. All 95 documents on it are in this database, as of the last check on Sept. 25, 2026. We do not include the OLR Past Agreements page (two citywide agreements with successors).
  • 20 documents from elsewhere, described under coverage: three fire and nurse agreements and five memoranda from OLR's download server, and 12 CUNY agreements.

If a stated term appears expired (for example, the Patrolmen's Benevolent Association's 2017-2025 agreement), the contract generally stays in force under New York State's Triborough Amendment, which keeps public-sector contract terms in effect until a successor is reached. Expirations are flagged but do not mean a contract has lapsed. The rule no longer applies once a successor exists: the NYSNA 2019-2023 agreement in this database has been replaced by a 2023 contract whose text has not been published.

Pipeline

  1. Inventory. scripts/inventory.py scrapes the OLR Recent Agreements page, finds every PDF link, and assigns each one an ID, label, source URL, and stated term years.
  2. Download. scripts/scrape.py downloads each PDF to data/pdfs/<id>.pdf.
  3. Extract. scripts/extract.py processes each PDF page-by-page:
    • Tables are detected and rendered as pipe-delimited markdown so column boundaries survive into the search corpus. (Naive PDF text extraction tends to flatten columns into a left-to-right wall of words; this preserves wage schedules, longevity tables, etc.)
    • Multi-column pages are detected via character-position analysis and split into left/right columns before extraction, so reading order is preserved.
    • Each page is scored on word count and alphabetic-character ratio. If a page falls below threshold (because the source PDF has no text layer), the page is rendered at 300 dpi and run through macOS Vision OCR via the ocrmac Python binding. Vision-based OCR handles real-world contract pages well — including stamps, signatures, and side-by-side tables.
    • OCR'd pages are flagged with the OCR badge in the UI so users know the text was reconstructed and may have minor errors. Pages whose PDF already carries a hidden OCR text layer from the city's scanner pass this threshold and are not re-OCR'd or flagged; about 570 pages in 18 documents fall in this group, and some of that embedded text is badly garbled (see limitations).
  4. Segment. scripts/segment.py splits each contract into clauses using article/section heading regexes (Article I, Section 1, all-caps headings, and numbered headings). Each clause carries an article, section, heading, page number, and OCR flag. Text before the first detected heading is kept as a preamble clause, table-of-contents lines are ignored, and any contract where heading detection covers less than 70% of the text is indexed page by page instead — so letter-style agreements without formal headings are fully searchable.
  5. Clean headings. scripts/clean_headings.py relabels "headings" that are not section titles: signers' names ("RENEE CAMPION"), letterhead, the caption block of each Comptroller's consent determination and table column labels ("MONTHLY ACCRUAL"). They become "Signatures," "Side letter," "Caption" or "(continued)" under the previous real heading. About 840 of 2,220 clause headings were relabeled. No text, page or permalink changes, and the original heading stays searchable.
  6. Tag. scripts/tag.py applies a topic taxonomy via keyword regex against each clause: wages, longevity, overtime, holidays, vacation, sick leave, parental leave, health & welfare, pension, grievance, discipline, layoff, hours, shift differential, uniform allowance, training, safety, no-strike, management rights, work rules, union security, recognition, promotion, telework, anti-discrimination, workforce composition. A clause can carry multiple topics.
  7. Index. The frontend loads data/clauses.json and builds a FlexSearch full-text index in the browser. No server-side query — everything runs client-side. Searches are union-acronym aware: a query containing an acronym (PBA, DC37, CIR, …) also searches the union's full name, and vice versa, so results don't depend on which form a given contract happens to use.

Term dates and status

Each document's term is taken from the document itself, not from its title or file name: the exact start and end dates, with the sentence and page they come from (data/term_dates.json; hover over a date on the site to see the wording). 97 of the 100 documents state a term. Three do not: the Sergeants and Detectives unit letters and the Uniformed Officers Coalition Economic Agreement, whose terms run 60 months from each unit's previous agreement; for those the site shows the years only.

The documents' own dates corrected six sets of years listed by OLR: CWA Local 1180 (runs to June 12, 2027), Local 237 Parking Control Specialists (May 10, 2027), Metal Work Mechanic (ended Nov. 2, 2025), SEIU Local 621 Supervisor of Mechanics (ended Nov. 18, 2025), the Sanitation Chiefs unit agreement (Jan. 1, 2023 to Feb. 29, 2028) and the Locksmiths determination (Dec. 29, 2020 to Jan. 28, 2026). Titles still follow OLR's listing.

Status on the site compares the end date with today. A contract past its end date generally stays in force under the Triborough Amendment until a successor is reached. Where a later document continues an expired one "as modified," or a successor exists but is unpublished, the status says so.

How documents are grouped by union

The home page groups every document by the union that signed it, taking each union's name from the document's own text. Two unions were previously mislabeled on this site and are corrected: Local 246 is Service Employees International Union (SEIU) Local 246, not part of DC 37, and Local 211 is the Allied Building Inspectors, International Union of Operating Engineers (IUOE) Local 211. The two joint motor grader and tractor operator agreements, signed by SEIU Local 246 and IUOE Local 15 together, appear under both. The grouping is kept by hand in data/unions.json.

What these documents are (and aren't)

Most of what the Office of Labor Relations publishes is not a complete contract. Of the 115 documents in this database, 74 expressly state that an underlying agreement remains in force and change only the terms written in them.

Those underlying agreements are public records, and many are published — but they are not linked from the amendments that depend on them. The Recent Agreements page lists only the current bargaining round, but OLR posts earlier rounds on separate pages: 2017-2021 Agreements (about 105 documents), 2010-2017 Agreements (about 140), Civilian Contracts (older, about 70) and Uniformed Contracts (about 30). The Past Agreements page itself holds only two citywide agreements. A scan of these pages on Sept. 25, 2026, followed by a document-by-document check, found the most recent earlier agreement for all but two of the amendments in this database (see below); many of those earlier documents are themselves amendments of a still earlier full contract. Earlier agreements can be reached in at least five ways:

  • OLR's separate Uniformed Contracts page. nyc.gov/site/olr/labor/labor-uniformed-contracts.page carries 30 older agreements, memoranda and reopeners for the police, fire, sanitation and correction unions, mostly from 2002 to 2012, identified by collective bargaining unit (CBU) number. None of them appear on the Recent Agreements page, and the current unit agreements do not link to them. They are the last full texts OLR published for those units, not necessarily the immediate predecessors. Where this database can identify the unit, each document links to the most recent full text.
  • Unlinked on the city's own server. Other agreements remain live under nyc.gov/assets/olr/downloads/pdf/collectivebargaining/ without appearing on any OLR page at all. The Sergeants Benevolent Association's 2018-2021 memorandum of agreement, for example, is a 4.7 MB PDF that resolves today but is not linked from any OLR index — you can only reach it if you already know the file name.
  • From the unions. Many unions post their own contracts; the Committee of Interns and Residents and PSC-CUNY texts in this corpus come from union or CUNY copies for exactly that reason.
  • Outside databases. The Empire Center's SeeThroughNY contracts database hosts downloadable New York public-sector agreements, including New York City ones.
  • By records request. Executed agreements are public records obtainable under the state Freedom of Information Law (FOIL).

So the accurate characterization is a findability problem rather than a secrecy one: a reader who starts from an amendment on the OLR site has no link from that document to the agreement it modifies, even though the city publishes earlier agreements on its archive pages. Those earlier agreements are not yet in this database.

This database supplies that path where it can. Of the 74 amendments:

  • 57 link the most recent earlier agreement for the same unit, as the city published it. Each match was checked by reading both documents: the earlier one must cover the same unit and union, and its term must end where this one begins (51 end exactly the day before). Where there is a gap or the earlier document covers only part of the unit, the contract page says so. The checked matches, with a quoted line from each earlier document, are in data/predecessors_verified.json. Many of these earlier documents are themselves amendments; where the full contract behind one was found, it is linked too.
  • 4 (DC 37, the PBA, the UFT and the Sergeants) link an older full agreement found earlier by scripts/build_predecessors.py.
  • 11 have their full current terms in another document in this database: the five CUNY memoranda and their full agreements, the four trade memoranda and their consent determinations, the sanitation workers' economic agreement and their executed contract, and the PSC memorandum and the 2017-2023 PSC agreement.
  • 2 have no earlier agreement available. The city's link for the earlier Boilermaker agreement serves a copy of the Blacksmith agreement, and the Masters, Mates and Pilots agreement between 2018 and 2021 is not posted.

Linked earlier agreements are not in this database and are not searchable here; they open as PDFs on the city's site.

Every document is classified by scripts/classify_docs.py, which reads the document text rather than trusting its file name, and records the verbatim sentence that justified the amendment flag. The four categories: The document's own title decides first: one headed "Memorandum of Agreement" is an amendment even if its text mentions "the predecessor Consent Determination," and one that opens "This INDENTURE" is a wage indenture. That rule, added Sept. 25, 2026, corrected the type of 14 documents.

  • Full agreements (14) — self-contained contracts with a complete article structure. Median length about 131,000 characters.
  • Consent determinations (26) — prevailing-wage determinations for skilled-trade titles under state Labor Law section 220. Their terms are agreed between OLR and the union to settle a wage complaint and issued as a determination rather than signed as a contract. Most carry a full Appendix A of time and leave benefits. Median about 52,000 characters. Wage indentures, an older form of the same instrument, are counted here.
  • Amendments, or memoranda of agreement (66) — documents that change specific economic terms and leave the rest of a prior agreement intact. Median about 5,100 characters. This includes the Uniformed Officers Coalition Economic Agreement, which changes the terms of 11 unions\' agreements.
  • Uniformed unit agreements (9) — short letters executed under the Uniformed Officers Coalition Economic Agreement, which supplies their wage increases. Median about 3,100 characters.

One consequence is visible in the topic tags: wage provisions appear in all 115 documents, but grievance and arbitration provisions appear in only 33. Every one of these bargaining units has a grievance procedure; for most of the rest, it lives in an underlying agreement that is not in this database. (Management rights are a different case: in New York City they are set largely by statute, in Administrative Code section 12-307(b), rather than by contract.)

Coverage beyond the Recent Agreements page

Twenty documents come from outside the Recent Agreements page. Each is marked in the data with its source ("olr-direct," "olr-unlinked" or "cuny-direct"). Fifteen of them were added on Sept. 25, 2026 after a scan of official sources (data/source-scan-2026-09-25/) and processed with scripts/add_documents.py, which runs the same extraction, segmentation, tagging and reflow steps on the new documents only.

  • UFA (about 8,500 firefighters, International Association of Fire Fighters Local 94). The most recent UFA agreement OLR has published is the 2017-2020 memorandum of agreement (MOA). The UFA signed the 2022-2027 Uniformed Officers Coalition Economic Agreement, which sets its raises for the round that began Aug. 1, 2020, but its successor unit agreements have not been published.
  • UFOA (about 2,400 fire lieutenants, captains, chiefs and other officers, Local 854). The most recent published agreement is the 2018-2021 Fire Officers Agreement. The UFOA also signed the coalition agreement; its successor unit agreement has not been published.
  • NYSNA (about 8,000 registered nurses at NYC Health + Hospitals and mayoral agencies). The most recent published agreement is the 2019-2023 Staff Nurses Agreement. It has been replaced by a five-and-a-half-year contract settled in July 2023, whose text has not been published.
  • PSC (about 30,000 faculty and professional staff at CUNY, American Federation of Teachers Local 2334). Both the 2023-2027 MOA and the underlying 2017-2023 agreement are included, from CUNY's labor relations page.
  • CUNY non-faculty staff (added Sept. 25, 2026). The full 2021-2027 Blue Collar and White Collar agreements, covering DC 37 locals, Teamsters Local 237, SEIU Local 300, IATSE Local 306 and NYSNA nurses at CUNY; the Educational Opportunity Centers and Student Center agreements (DC 37 Locals 384 and 1597); and the 2023-2028 Theatre agreement with IATSE Local One. Each comes with the memorandum of agreement that produced it.
  • Five OLR memoranda with no link from any OLR page (added Sept. 25, 2026), found at their addresses on OLR's download server: the Laborers (DC 37 Local 924), Sheet Metal Worker (SEIU Local 246), Maintenance Workers (Teamsters Local 237) and Carpenters memoranda that accompany the consent determinations of the same names, and the sanitation workers' 2022-2028 Memorandum of Economic Agreement. A sixth, an earlier version of the CSA memorandum, was left out because the amended version in the database replaces it.

Known missing documents. The home page lists every document known to be missing, with evidence: see the list. It covers two kinds of gap. First, current agreements that exist but have not been published: the NYSNA 2023 contract and the UFA and UFOA successor unit agreements. Second, the underlying agreements that 63 amendments keep in force but that are not in this database; 61 of those link the earlier agreement the city published. The affected documents carry a pink "missing" badge throughout the site. The list is maintained by hand in data/missing.json and was last checked Sept. 25, 2026.

OCR accuracy and corrections

Because most source PDFs are scanned images, the OCR text can contain misread words. Two kinds of cleanup have been applied:

  • Token corrections. Multiple review passes (a fuzzy edit-distance scanner against a corpus-derived wordlist, followed by manual review) have fixed roughly 500 high-confidence single-token misreads — for example employce → employee, Depuly → Deputy, earnable mis-scanned as carnable. Every correction is recorded in data/ocr-corrections-batch*.json for auditability; most are applied corpus-wide, and some are scoped to the specific contract(s) where the misread occurs. The seven worst-quality contracts were re-OCR'd from scratch via Google Cloud Vision. All corrections and the Vision re-OCR are applied to the canonical extracted text layer (data/text/, via scripts/repair_canonical_text.py), so the search index, the Markdown exports and the Gemini Notebook bundles are all rebuilt from the same corrected source.
  • Complete-text segmentation. Clause detection is heading-based, which used to silently drop body text in short letter-style agreements that have no Article/Section headings. The segmenter now captures text before the first detected heading, ignores table-of-contents lines, and — if heading detection still covers less than 70% of a document — falls back to indexing the contract page by page, so every agreement's full text is searchable.
  • Text-native re-sourcing. Where a union publishes a clean, text-native copy of the same agreement, the full text is taken from that copy instead of OCR — eliminating scan errors entirely. The CIR 2021-2027 contract was re-sourced from the union's published final contract (dropping it from 52 OCR pages to 2), and the PSC-CUNY 2023-2027 MOA from the PSC's published MOA (from 55 OCR pages to 0). The OLR/CUNY scan remains the canonical executed version linked from each contract's detail page; only the searchable text was upgraded.

Limitations

  • OCR is not perfect. Scanned pages may have spelling errors that would not appear in a clean text PDF. The OCR flag marks pages this project OCR'd, but about 570 more pages carry text from an OCR layer embedded in the city's PDF and are not flagged. Some of that embedded text is unreadable, including parts of the NYSNA, UFOA and 2001-2021 citywide agreements. Salary tables are often stored column by column, so a rate may not sit next to its title. Always verify by clicking through to the source PDF.
  • Heading detection is heuristic. If a contract uses unusual numbering or all-caps body text, segmentation may merge or split clauses incorrectly. The page number on each clause anchors it back to the source PDF for verification.
  • Topic tags are keyword-based. A clause that mentions "discipline" in passing while really being about safety will get a discipline tag. Tags are a navigation aid, not a legal classification.
  • Side letters. Some agreements have side letters or memoranda of understanding that aren't in the linked PDF. Where OLR publishes these as separate documents, they appear as separate entries.
  • Headcounts appear only where the figure was checked against a named primary source: a NYC Mayor's Office settlement announcement, the NYC Council Finance Division's October 2025 NYPD and FDNY workforce reports, NYSNA or CUNY. That is 14 bargaining units; each contract page quotes the source. Headcounts are for the workers covered by the specific agreement, not a union's total membership. The 1199 SEIU figure (about 2,500) is not shown because its only source is a 2014 announcement. Other units have no headcount yet.
  • Other employers. The Recent Agreements page includes major agreements covering the Department of Education (DOE), the New York City Housing Authority (NYCHA) and NYC Health + Hospitals. Apart from the five documents described under coverage, agreements not posted by OLR are not here. CUNY also posts current agreements for its non-faculty staff (blue collar, white collar, educational opportunity centers, student centers and theater staff) on its labor contracts page; these are not yet in this database. Neither are six current OLR documents that sit on its download server without a link from any OLR page, including memoranda that accompany the Carpenters, Laborers, Maintenance Workers and Sheet Metal Worker consent determinations.
  • NYC Health + Hospitals (H+H) is partially covered. H+H is a public benefit corporation that sometimes negotiates contracts directly with unions instead of through OLR, and not all H+H agreements get posted to OLR. The database includes Doctors Council (about 500 city-employed physicians at H+H, the Department of Health and Mental Hygiene and the medical examiner's office; attending physicians employed by H+H affiliate groups bargain separately), 1199 SEIU (about 2,500 non-physician health care workers in its citywide City and H+H unit) and the Committee of Interns and Residents (CIR, resident physicians). DC 37 locals such as Local 420 (Municipal Hospital Employees Union) and Local 768 (Health Services) cover several thousand more H+H workers under the DC 37 economic agreement, which is in the database. NYSNA's current contract, covering about 8,000 registered nurses, is a known gap: only its replaced 2019-2023 agreement is here.

Wage tracker — verification

The wage pattern tracker shows curated general wage increase (GWI) schedules for 13 large contracts. scripts/verify_wages.py checks every step against the contract text: each effective date must appear next to its percentage. Contract pages show a wage schedule only for the 10 contracts where every step passes; the ratification bonus shown with six of them was checked the same way. The badges on the tracker show where the other figures come from:

  • Verified — both percentages and dates were quoted from the contract.
  • Set by parent (UOCEA) — the Sergeants Benevolent Association (SBA) and Detectives' Endowment Association (DEA) unit agreements incorporate the Uniformed Officers Coalition Economic Agreement (UOCEA); percentages are the parent's.
  • PDF appendix — UFT's salary tables live in Appendix A; we link to the source PDF rather than extract percentages from it. The NYC Mayor's Office gives UFT's increases as 3.00/3.00/3.00/3.25/3.50%, or 16.77% compounded.

The $3,000 ratification bonus appears only in the civilian-pattern contracts; the uniformed pattern doesn't include it. PBA's prior 2017-2025 settlement also has no $3,000 bonus.

Natural-language Q&A

For natural-language questions across the corpus — "compare the discipline procedures for sanitation workers and teachers," "which contracts have parental leave," "what's the longest grievance timeline" — use the companion Gemini Notebook, which has been loaded with the same Markdown corpus this site is built from. Gemini Notebook provides citations back to the source documents and runs on Google's infrastructure (free for users with a Google account).

This site itself is search-only by design — it doesn't call any LLM API at query time, so it stays free to host and free to use. The two tools complement each other: use the search/topic-pivot here for keyword-precise lookups and citations, use Gemini Notebook for cross-document synthesis questions.

Markdown export

Every contract is also published as a standalone Markdown file under /data/markdown/<contract-id>.md, with YAML frontmatter for metadata, page numbers and OCR flags inline, and tables preserved as pipe-delimited rows. A single ZIP bundle of all 100 contracts (~1.2 MB) is also available, plus a per-contract download button on every contract detail page. Built by scripts/export_markdown.py from the same source data the search index uses.

Refresh

The corpus is regenerated by re-running the inventory + scrape + extract + segment + tag pipeline. The footer of the home page shows the build timestamp.
